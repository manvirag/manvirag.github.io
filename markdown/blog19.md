### how different muscles work, and what actually trains them

This is a personal gym note. The aim is simple: understand **which muscle group does what**, **which exercise hits it**, and **what form change shifts the load**. Not a diet plan. Not a coach program. Just the technical map, with few exercise ( we can have lots more its just mental model. ). Also muscle group on high level ( we not reading biology so that we go that deep dive this is for gym only. )

Muscle is broken during a workout and rebuilt after. The exercise only decides *which* tissue you stressed. Form decides whether you actually stressed that tissue, or just moved weight with momentum.

Most of the volume should be **compound** (multi-joint), then **isolation** to fill gaps.

### Legs

Treat the lower body as three jobs: **glutes (hips)**, **thigh (quad + hamstring)**, **calves**.

#### glutes (hips)

Glutes have multiple parts. For training, treat them as one group that extends the hip.

- **Barbell hip thrust** — hip extension, glute-primary.
- **Bulgarian split squat (glute focus)** — same pattern as a split squat, but the rear foot is on a bench. It loads more than a regular split squat. Beginners can start with a split squat on the floor.
  - form matters because the same movement can be **quad-focus (torso more upright)** or **glute-focus (slightly more hip bend)**.
  - [bulgarian / glute form](https://youtube.com/shorts/tdOk9XkzGVc)
  - [quad vs glute version](https://youtube.com/shorts/qHDB5HkOrrs)
  - [bulgarian vs split squat](https://youtube.com/shorts/s4DmteZRXZg)
- **Romanian deadlift (RDL)** — hamstring + glute. A bit more hip bend → more glute. Less bend → more hamstring.
  - [rdl form](https://youtube.com/shorts/hu3jRvTc_po)
- **Squat** — can be glute-focus or quad-focus depending on stance. Detail below.
  - [squat form](https://youtube.com/shorts/UOhEq8-6iNk)
- **Lunge (with a bent / hip-hinge posture)** — also hits glute + quad.
  - lunge vs split squat: both hit glute and quad. Lunge steps back (or forward). Split squat stays in one place. A lunge is basically a split squat plus the extra step.
  - [lunge vs split squat](https://youtube.com/shorts/uE_NeT2D0lo)
  - [lunge form](https://youtube.com/shorts/EIycwKi4Mfk)

#### thigh group

**Hamstring (back of thigh)**

- **RDL** again. It is different from a conventional deadlift: in a deadlift you sit the hips down more; in an RDL the hips travel back. Not much knee bend if the goal is hamstring.

**Quadriceps (front of thigh)**

Four heads. The three you actually see: **vastus lateralis** (outer), **rectus femoris** (middle / crossing the hip), **vastus medialis** (inner tear-drop).

![](/markdown/blog19/quads.png)

- **Leg extension** — isolation. Hits all four heads of the quad.
- **Dumbbell bulgarian split squat (upright torso)** — more quad than the glute version. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/qHDB5HkOrrs)
- **Squat** — hits both quad and hamstring. Variations matter more than “barbell vs dumbbell”:
  - dumbbell vs barbell / smith: dumbbell is easier to start with, limited by grip, relatively less load.
  - **stance / form (the useful part):**
    - feet a bit forward + a bit wide → more **glute**
    - feet under you + a bit wide → more **quad**
    - wider stance → **adductor** (inner thigh) + glute
    - feet close together and under you → **outer quads**

Front squat vs back squat is another lever: front squat is more quad-dominant, back squat keeps more hamstring / posterior chain in the lift.

#### calves

At a practical level, one pattern is enough: **calf raise**. Different machines, same joint.

- full stretch at the bottom, pause, then lift onto the toes.
- keep the foot half on a plate / step so the heel can go below the toes. Pause so the calf takes the load, not momentum.
- can also be done with a bench and a dumbbell / barbell. [example](https://youtube.com/shorts/ar8nav0jGoE)

### Shoulders

Three heads: **front**, **side (lateral)**, **rear**.

**All three (mostly front + side)**

- **Seated DB shoulder press** — primary front and side delts. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/DSrUsmyUUbs)

**Side (lateral)**

- **Lateral raise** — isolation for width. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/Kl3LEzQ5Zqs)
- slight **forward lean** + slightly **bent elbows** isolates the side delt better and is easier on the joint than a fully upright, locked-elbow swing.
  - torso: ~15–20° lean at the hips. fully upright tends to dump the work into front delt and upper traps.
  - elbows: ~20–30° bend, **fixed** through the rep. locked-straight arms lengthen the lever and invite shrugging.
  - raise in the scapular plane (slightly in front of the body, not strictly out to the sides) to reduce impingement.
  - cue: push the dumbbells **out toward the walls**, lead with elbows, not hands.
  - cables keep tension more even than dumbbells if available.

**Rear / back delt**

- **Bent-over DB rear delt fly** (reverse fly). Simplest gym version: **seated bent-over DB rear delt fly** on a bench.
- [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/3icYcehmnsY)

### biceps

[overview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20ibpB635Rw)

It has outer, inner, and a middle story. Don’t over-split it. Pick lifts that cover the whole arm.

![](/markdown/blog19/biceps.png)

- **Outer head:** incline dumbbell curl. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/XhIsIcjIbCw)
- **Short head:** underhand lat pulldown works, but with dumbbells:
  - concentration curls
  - **single-arm DB preacher curls** — [form 1](https://youtube.com/shorts/Mg0NnlF5NZQ), [form 2](https://youtube.com/shorts/LUBgyjKCRWQ)
- **Brachialis** (side-arm thickness, sits under the biceps): **dumbbell hammer curl**. Also hits forearms.

### triceps

[overview](https://youtube.com/shorts/8IK6BkC0lWE)

Three heads.

![](/markdown/blog19/triceps.png)

- **Long head (back / mass):** largest piece, gives the upper arm size.
  - **overhead DB tricep extension**. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/AYqg9S5FrUU)
- **Lateral head (outer horseshoe):** the shape you see from the side.
  - **cable rope pushdown**
- **Medial head (lower base, near elbow):** thickness + joint support.
  - **reverse-grip cable pushdown**. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/Y83jP-sty6E)

### forearms

Three useful buckets.

![](/markdown/blog19/forearms.png)

- **Brachioradialis** (top thickness near the elbow): hammer curl covers this. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/8H5oWMNWWeQ)
- **Flexors** (palm side, hanging size): **seated DB wrist curl** — palm up, curl the weight. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/fpROYtdvYBQ)
- **Extensors** (wrist stability / top of forearm): **seated DB reverse wrist curl**. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/cRLJ86m00cU)

Do wrist curl and reverse wrist curl as a superset.

### Chest

Upper, middle, lower. [overview](https://youtu.be/MxnzcssW-tk)

For a lean athletic frame (think swimmer / cricket-athlete look, not bodybuilder shelf), **upper + middle** do most of the work. Direct lower-chest isolation is optional. Rough split in my notes: ~50% upper, ~45% middle, skip dedicated lower if the bench setup can’t decline.

![](/markdown/blog19/chest-1.png)

- **Upper:** 30° **incline DB press** (primary). Or incline smith press.
- **Middle:** **flat DB press** or flat smith press.
- **Lower:** decline DB press / glute-bridge DB press. Needs a decline bench. Lower-chest work fills the bottom slope; not required for the frame above.

### Abs

Three parts you actually train. There is a fourth piece that helps the V-shape below the navel; don’t overthink it.

![](/markdown/blog19/abs.png)

**Upper abs**

- cable crunches. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/K2m0jj6RfYg)
- decline crunches (if the machine exists)
- planks

**Lower abs**

- knee raises / hanging leg raises (if you have a bar)
- **leg raise** on floor or flat bench. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/PkLYH3uRtSs) — a sloppy version becomes more hip-flexor than abs.
- **reverse crunch** — legs go past ~90° toward the chest (~120°). More efficient than a plain floor leg raise.
  - [leg raise vs reverse crunch](https://youtube.com/shorts/V8VT99M2sNk)

**Obliques**

- russian twist
- bicycle crunches. [form](https://youtube.com/shorts/cFDS2S6Vqis)
- cross-body mountain climbers

### Back

This is the messy one. A lot of muscles, one visual job: **width (V-taper)** + **thickness**.

**Seated cable row (~25%)** — horizontal pull. Thickness through mid-back / traps / lats, biceps and rear delts help.

**Lat pulldown and pull-ups**

- **wide grip (~50%)** — upper-outer back, teres major, the “wings”. Width.
- **close grip (~25%)** — lower lats, mid-back, biceps. [close-grip note](https://youtu.be/-JbIJcqZ2uk)

### Weekly split ( There are a lot different way, this is one of them . You can learn more about different. Need to refine more, in progress. )

Push / pull / legs, then a second upper and lower. Two rest days.

#### day 1 — push (chest, shoulder, tricep)

V-taper “cap”: incline chest and side delts get priority, but the heavy chest compound still goes first while energy is high.

- flat DB press
- incline dumbbell press
- seated DB shoulder press
- lateral raise (use cable if possible)
- reverse-grip cable pushdown *(optional)*
- overhead DB tricep extension
- rope pushdown (lateral head)
- abs

#### day 2 — pull + forearms (back, rear delt, biceps)

Wider back makes the waist look smaller. That is most of the V.

- lat pulldown (wide)
- seated cable row
- seated bent-over DB rear delt fly / cable single-arm rear delt fly
- dumbbell hammer curl
- incline DB curl
- preacher curl
- seated DB wrist curl superset reverse wrist curl, cross
- no abs

#### day 3 — legs + abs

Posterior chain + a core circuit.

- Squat — 3 × 8–10
- RDL — 3 × 8–10
- bulgarian split squat (glute focus) — 2 × 10–12 / or lunge
- calf raise — 4 × 15
- russian twist — 3 × 20 total
- cross-body mountain climbers — 3 × 45s

#### day 4 — rest

#### day 5 — upper (shoulders + lat width again) -> Need to update more.

- incline dumbbell press
- lat pulldown (wide) — 3 × 8–10
- lateral raise
- shoulder press
- incline dumbbell curl
- single-arm DB preacher curl
- cable rope pushdown

#### day 6 — lower + abs

- squat — 3 × 6–8
- bulgarian split squat (glute focus) — 3 × 8–10 / leg
- leg extension — 3 × 12–15
- calf raise — 4 × 15
- plank — 3 × 45–60s
- cable crunch — 3 × 12–15

#### day 7 — rest
