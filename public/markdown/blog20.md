### The Formula: 5 Laws of Success

Notes from the book by Albert-László Barabási.

We usually think that if someone is talented and works hard, they will become successful. But that's not always how things work.

You can be good at something and still not get the recognition you deserve. These five laws explain why.

---

### 1. Performance drives success, but when performance can’t be measured, our networks drive success

When your performance can be measured, your work speaks for itself. But when it's difficult to judge someone's ability, people often rely on their network, reputation, and recommendations.

**Example:** In a running race, the fastest person wins. But in a company, two engineers can be equally good, and the one whose work is known to the manager might get promoted first.

**Takeaway:** Don't just do good work. Make sure the right people know about it.

---

### 2. Performance is bounded, but success is unbounded

There is a limit to how good you can become at something, but there is almost no limit to how successful you can become. A small difference in performance can lead to a huge difference in success.

**Example:** Two authors write equally good books. One sells 1,000 copies, while the other sells 10 lakh copies because their book reaches a much larger audience. The second author isn't necessarily 1,000 times better, but their success is 1,000 times greater.

**Takeaway:** Being good at your work is important, but how many people discover and appreciate your work can make a huge difference to your success. You don't have to be 10 times better than others to become 10 times more successful. Sometimes, the difference is how many people know about and benefit from your work.

---

### 3. Future success is dictated by previous success multiplied by the fitness of your idea or product.

Once you've achieved something, people start trusting you more. They are more likely to give you another opportunity. But you still need to deliver good work.

**Example:** Two founders approach an investor with equally good ideas. The founder who has already built a successful startup may find it easier to get funding because they have a proven track record.

**Takeaway:** Your past success can open doors for your next success. Use it to build further.

---

### 4. While team success requires diversity and balance, a single individual will receive credit for the group’s achievements.

A team performs better when people with different skills work together. But when the team achieves something big, people often give most of the credit to one person instead of recognising everyone's contribution.

**Example:** A startup succeeds because of its engineers, designers, sales team, and founder. But when the company becomes famous, most people know the founder's name, not the names of everyone who helped build it.

**Takeaway:** Great achievements are often the result of teamwork, but recognition doesn't always get shared equally. Build strong teams, contribute meaningfully, and make sure people's efforts are recognised.

---

### 5. With persistence success can come at any time

You might work hard for years without getting the results you want. That doesn't mean you're not capable of succeeding. Sometimes, the right opportunity comes much later.

**Example:** You keep writing technical blogs, but hardly anyone reads them. After writing 20 articles, one finally reaches the right audience and brings you a great job opportunity.

**Takeaway:** Don't stop just because your efforts haven't paid off yet. Keep improving and putting your work out there.
